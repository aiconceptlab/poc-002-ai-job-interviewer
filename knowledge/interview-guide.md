# Interview practice guide

Use a real non-sensitive example, a school or volunteer project, or an explicitly fictional scenario. The size of the project is less important than making your own actions clear.

A useful shape is: context → decision/action → outcome → lesson. This is a guide, not a rigid script. A concise answer can be strong. Specific qualitative outcomes count; numbers are not always available or useful.

## Four scoring criteria
- **Relevance:** address the question and explain your own role.
- **Structure:** make the context, action and outcome easy to follow.
- **Evidence:** support claims with concrete actions, examples or results.
- **Reflection:** explain a lesson, limitation or next step.

Each uses the same 1–5 anchors in lib/questions.ts. All have equal weight. No employment pass/fail threshold is defined.

## Before you request feedback
Correct transcription mistakes and remove confidential details. The evaluator sees the selected question and exactly the answer you submit. It does not see your face, voice, CV, conversation history or other answers.

## After you receive feedback
Read the supporting quote and explanation. Decide whether the feedback is justified. If a result is stated, explain how you know it happened. If a lesson is vague, say what you would do differently next time. Keep your own story truthful; do not invent metrics to satisfy a score.

The example in lib/sample.ts is fictional and its feedback was authored for demonstration. It is not a real applicant record or a measured benchmark.
